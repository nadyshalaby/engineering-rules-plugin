# 15.27 [reference] The pattern vocabulary

## Names to think with, not a library to paste from

A shared vocabulary of interface patterns, so a composition plan (15.22) can name what a
section is instead of describing it, a review can name what went wrong, and a builder can
reach for the right pattern when the read (15.20) calls for it. Read the family you need at
Phase 3; never the whole file by reflex. Implementations are the project's own, written
against its spec (15.3); the motion patterns here obey 15.25 and the layout patterns obey
15.22.

---

### Composition axes, chosen per page and per section

Before the patterns, the axes a page is composed on. Pick one value per axis per section,
vary across the page, and keep one brand world (15.31, consistency). The composition plan
records the choice.

**Theme paradigm** (per page, one): pristine light (off-white, paper, sharp dark text);
deep dark (charcoal, graphite, glow only when justified); bold studio solid (one strong
colour field: oxblood, royal blue, forest, vermilion, emerald, with crisp contrasting UI);
quiet premium neutral (bone, sand, taupe, stone, smoke).

**Background character** (per page, one dominant): a subtle technical grid or dotted field;
a solid field with soft ambient depth; full-bleed cinematic imagery with contrast control;
a tactile textured surface.

**Typography character** (per page, one): clean grotesk; refined grotesk; expressive display;
compressed statement type; editorial serif with a sans; Swiss rational hierarchy. The
direction's pairing (15.4) usually decides this.

**Hero scale** (per page, one, decided rather than split): giant statement (massive type,
large image, dominant viewport); mid editorial (balanced type and image, cinematic but not
screen-filling); mini minimalist (small logo, short statement, thin CTA, mostly negative
space; mini means confident restraint, not weak).

**Section system** (per page, one dominant): modular bento rhythm; alternating editorial
blocks; poster-like stacked storytelling; gallery-led cadence; Swiss grid discipline;
asymmetric premium marketing flow.

**Composition anchor** (per section; at least three different anchors across a page; the
same anchor never three times running): centered statement; top-left lead with support
bottom-right; bottom-left text over a background image; bottom-right CTA cluster;
left-third caption beside a right two-thirds visual (the classic, used sparingly, never
twice in a row); right-third caption beside a left two-thirds visual (the inverted classic);
centered low (text in the lower 40% over a hero image); off-grid editorial offset; stacked
center (label, headline, sub, CTA, all centered, ultra minimal); image-as-canvas with text
in a clean safe area.

**Background mode** (per section; never the same mode more than three times running; a
non-minimal page carries at least one full-bleed, duotone or atmospheric section and at
least one mini section): solid surface with an inline asset; subtle texture, paper or grid;
full-bleed image with a tonal overlay; editorial side image (50/50, 60/40, 40/60,
invertible); image as the whole visual with overlaid text; flat colour block with a small
product crop; cinematic tonal gradient; atmospheric photo with a strong single-tone grade;
duotone image; soft radial vignette with a product crop; micro-noise gradient over a solid;
colour-blocked diptych. A minimalist brief suspends the variety rule; restraint is the
design.

**CTA variation** (per section, varied at least once per page while the primary action stays
unmistakable): classic primary pill; outline or ghost; underlined inline link with an arrow;
banner-style full-width CTA; oversized headline with a tiny CTA hint; a CTA as a caption
under a strong visual.

**Narrative spine** (per page, one, threaded through visuals and copy): artifact or
collectible (proof, specimen, treasured object); journey or pilgrimage (directional flow,
waypoints); tool or precision instrument (machined detail, calibrated UI); living system or
garden (growth, branching); stage or spotlight (theatrical contrast); archive or dossier
(indexed rows, understated authority).

**Second-read moment** (per page, exactly one, placed once, legible, aiding scan order or
brand recall): an asymmetric bleed that still respects hierarchy; one oversized numeral or
punctuation mark serving structure; a single material switch (paper against gloss or metal);
a narrow vertical side-rail note; a macro crop carrying brand colour. Never a gimmick for its
own sake.

### Hero paradigms

- **Asymmetric split hero.** Text on one side, asset on the other, generous space. The
  default for one strong asset beside one strong message; also the most overused generated
  hero, so it needs its reason (15.22).
- **Editorial manifesto hero.** Large type, no asset, almost a poster. Right when the message
  is the design.
- **Cinematic centered minimalist.** Centered statement, two CTAs, a full-bleed image with a
  dark radial wash. The other reflex; needs its reason.
- **Massive image-first hero.** The image dominates; restrained text sits in a safe area.
- **Editorial offset composition.** Text offset to one side, an image overlapping from the
  opposite corner.
- **Floating polaroid scatter.** Clustered, slightly rotated imagery around the copy;
  styled, not scrapbook-random.
- **Inline typography behemoth.** Small photographs set inline between the words of a giant
  headline as visual punctuation; images stack under the headline on mobile.
- **Video or media mask hero.** Type cut out as a mask over video.
- **Kinetic-type hero.** Animated typography as the primary visual (15.25 applies).
- **Curtain-reveal hero.** The hero parts on scroll.
- **Scroll-pinned hero.** The hero stays while content scrolls behind it.

### Navigation and menus

- **Island or floating pill nav.** A detached rounded bar with top margin, centered, width to
  content; on open, a hamburger that morphs into a cross and a full-screen glass overlay
  whose links rise in from a masked box with a short stagger.
- **Dock magnification.** Edge nav whose icons scale fluidly under the pointer.
- **Magnetic button.** Pulls toward the cursor, via motion values only (15.25).
- **Gooey menu.** Sub-items detach like viscous liquid.
- **Dynamic island.** A morphing pill for status and alerts.
- **Contextual radial menu.** A circular menu expanding at the click point.
- **Floating speed dial.** A FAB springing into a curved arc of secondary actions.
- **Mega-menu reveal.** A full-screen dropdown whose content stagger-fades.

### Layout and grids

- **Bento grid.** Asymmetric tile grouping with exact cell count and background diversity
  (15.22).
- **Pristine gapless bento.** Mathematically clean, no accidental gaps, large visual blocks
  beside small dense panels; `grid-auto-flow: dense`.
- **Masonry.** Staggered grid with no fixed row height.
- **Diagonal staggered square masonry.** Square blocks in a strong staggered rhythm; curated,
  not messy.
- **Chroma grid.** Borders or tiles with a slow animating gradient; `cyber-neon` territory.
- **Split-screen scroll.** Two halves sliding in opposite directions.
- **Sticky-stack sections.** Sections that pin and stack on scroll (recipe 1, 15.25).
- **Off-grid editorial layout.** Deliberate asymmetry and tension, still readable.
- **Vertical rhythm lines.** Fine rules and spacing that reinforce order; never clutter.
- **Colour-blocked diptych.** Two flat fields meeting; modernist.

### Cards and containers

- **Double bezel (nested enclosure).** A padded outer shell with a hairline ring and a large
  radius, holding an inner core with its own surface, an inset highlight and a
  concentrically smaller radius (outer radius minus the shell's padding). Reads as machined
  hardware; the concentric rule is binding wherever containers nest (15.22).
- **Button-in-button trailing icon.** A pill button whose trailing arrow sits inside its own
  small circular well flush with the button's inner edge; on hover the well drifts
  diagonally and scales slightly.
- **Parallax tilt card.** A 3D tilt tracking the pointer.
- **Spotlight border card.** A border that lights under the cursor.
- **Glass panel.** Frosted glass with an inner refraction border (15.21, 15.25).
- **Holographic foil card.** Iridescent shift on hover; rare, agency only.
- **Swipe stack.** A physical stack the user swipes away; native and touch.
- **Morphing modal.** A button that expands into its own dialog with a shared-element
  transition.
- **Faux OS window chrome.** A software mock inside a minimal frame with three small
  window-control circles; the honest way to frame a real screenshot.
- **Keystroke micro-UI.** Shortcuts rendered as physical keys with `kbd`, a hairline, a
  small radius and the mono face.
- **Featured-versus-rest disclosure.** Three or four hero specs as large tiles, the rest
  under one "full specifications" toggle (15.22, spec sheets).

### Scroll animations

- **Sticky scroll stack** (recipe 1, 15.25).
- **Horizontal scroll pan** (recipe 2, 15.25).
- **Scroll-reveal stagger** (recipe 3, 15.25).
- **Pinned title beside a scrolling gallery.** A section title pins left while elements
  scroll on the right.
- **Image scale-and-fade.** Images enter small and grow to full, then darken and fade as
  they leave.
- **Scrubbing text reveal.** Word opacity scrubs from faint to full as the reader scrolls.
- **Sequence scroll.** A video or image sequence tied to the scrollbar.
- **Zoom parallax.** A central image zooming on scroll.
- **Scroll progress path.** An SVG line drawing along scroll.
- **Liquid swipe transition.** A page transition like viscous liquid.

### Galleries and media

- **Dome gallery.** A 3D panoramic gallery.
- **Coverflow carousel.** A 3D carousel, center focused, edges angled.
- **Drag-to-pan grid.** A boundless draggable canvas.
- **Accordion image slider (hover accordion).** Narrow slices expanding on hover.
- **Turning polaroid arc.** Rotated images in an elegant arc.
- **Layered image crop frames.** Overlapping framed crops with a system of ratios.
- **Hover image trail.** Images popping along the pointer's path.
- **Glitch image.** RGB-channel shift on hover; `cyber-neon` only, never decorative.

### Typography and text

- **Kinetic marquee.** An endless text band reversing on scroll; at most one per page.
- **Text mask reveal.** Massive type as a window onto video.
- **Text scramble.** Character decoding on load or hover.
- **Circular text path.** Text along a spinning circle.
- **Gradient stroke animation.** Outlined text with a running stroke gradient.
- **Kinetic typography grid.** Letters dodging the cursor.
- **Variable-font animation.** Weight or width interpolated on scroll or hover.
- **Outline-to-fill transition.** Stroked text filling on entry or interaction.
- **Inline typography images.** Small rounded photographs set between words at type
  height.
- **ASCII framing and industrial markers.** `[ DELIVERY SYSTEMS ]`, `>>>`, `///`, `®` and
  `©` as geometry, crosshairs at grid intersections; `brutalist-mono` and `retro-terminal`
  only, and only where they organise real content (15.22).

### Micro-interactions and effects

- **Particle burst on success.** A CTA that shatters into particles.
- **Liquid pull-to-refresh.** Detaching droplets; native.
- **Skeleton shimmer.** A light reflection across placeholders.
- **Directional hover-aware button.** The fill enters from the cursor's side.
- **Ripple click.** A wave from the click point.
- **Animated SVG line drawing.** Vectors drawing themselves.
- **Mesh gradient background.** Organic slow blobs; allowed only as a single very slow,
  very low-opacity fixed layer, and never on `cyber-neon`'s adjacent cliché
  (`tell.visual.mesh-blob`).
- **Lens-blur depth.** Background UI blurred to focus a foreground action.
- **Grain overlay.** A fixed, non-interactive noise layer that breaks digital flatness
  (15.26).
- **Analog degradation.** Halftone, one-bit dither, CRT scanlines via a repeating gradient;
  `brutalist-mono` and `retro-terminal` at low amplitude.
- **Hairline grid trick.** `display: grid; gap: 1px` with contrasting parent and child
  surfaces yields razor-thin dividers with no border declarations.

### Native patterns

Large hero metric card; compact stat strip; modular collection grid; media carousel;
layered profile header; premium segmented control; bottom action sheet; framed product card
stack; progress ring; message bubble system; settings group cells; photo-led card strip;
sticky mini player; collection shelf; habit tracker block; checkout summary card; journal
entry card; achievement tile row. Chosen four at a time per set (15.31).

### Keeping a block library

A project that builds many pages may keep implementations of these patterns as a block
library beside its spec, at `docs/design/blocks/<category>/<name>.md`, one block per file.
Each block declares in frontmatter its `category`, its dial compatibility (a variance,
motion and density range from 15.20), a `when_to_use` line, a `not_for` line and its
`stack`; and carries in its body a visual sketch, its props API, a minimal working
implementation (static layout with a client leaf for motion), an explicit mobile collapse,
one motion variant per dial band with the reduced-motion fallback, its dark-mode notes, its
anti-patterns and its real-world references. A block on an official design system (15.21)
is named `<name>--<system>.md`. Every block passes the pre-flight (15.30) on its own before
it is added, and no block is freelanced outside this shape.
