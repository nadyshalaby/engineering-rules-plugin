# 15.22 [law] Page composition

## Scope, stated first

The spec (15.3) governs the system: tokens, roles, components, states. This file governs the
**page**: how sections are stacked, how a hero is built, how many labels a page may wear,
where the logos go. It is the layer generated pages get wrong even when every token is right.

**Binding in full on marketing surfaces**: landing pages, portfolios, about and pricing pages,
editorial and launch pages, anything whose job is a first impression. **Product UI**
(dashboards, admin, settings, data tables, editors, wizards) follows its spec and direction;
from this file it takes only the sections marked *every surface*. **Native screen sets** have
their own section at the end. The AI-tell catalog (15.24) carries the id for every ban
named here; the design scout (15.29) counts what can be counted; the pre-flight (15.30)
ticks the rest.

---

### The composition plan, before the first component

A page is planned in writing before it is built, in chat in quick mode and in the work-doc's
Approach in full mode. It is the honest form of the "design plan" some prompt frameworks
demand before code, and the pre-flight checks the built page against it.

```
## Composition plan
Read:      <the one-line read, 15.20>
Dials:     variance <n>, motion <n>, density <n> (<preset or reason>)
Direction: <slug>  (system: <package>, when 15.21 applies)
Hero:      <architecture from 15.27>, because <one reason against the read>
Sections (<n>):
  1 Hero            · <layout family> · <anchor> · <background mode> · visual: <slot>
  2 Trust bar       · logo wall       · centered · solid                · visual: 6 marks
  3 <name>          · <family>        · <anchor> · <background mode>    · visual: <slot>
  ...
Eyebrow budget: ceil(<n>/3) = <k>, spent on: <sections>
Image slots:    <count>, source: <gen tool | seeded placeholder | provided>, real assets needed at: <list>
Motion:         one orchestrated reveal on <section>; <each other animation> because <one sentence>
Rotated away from: <15.20>
```

A page whose plan lists eight sections in the same layout family is rejected at the plan,
which is cheaper than rejecting it at the review.

### The hero, the first viewport

- **It fits.** Headline at most 2 lines on desktop; subtext at most 20 words and at most 4
  lines; the CTAs visible without scrolling. Overflow is a font-size error or a copy-length
  error, never a reason to push the CTA below the fold. If the value proposition cannot be
  said in 20 words, the proposition is unclear, not the rule too tight.
- **Font scale is planned with the asset.** Six-plus-word headlines beside a large asset do
  not start at the largest display step. The sensible default range is the `display-lg`
  step on desktop; the `display-xl` step is for three-to-five-word headlines. A four-line
  headline is always a font-size error. Give the headline a wide enough container to flow;
  a narrow column is how a two-line headline becomes six.
- **Top padding is capped at roughly 6rem** on desktop. Past that the content floats halfway
  down the viewport and reads as a layout bug. More breathing room comes from scale, not
  from padding.
- **At most four text elements.** One of eyebrow, brand strip or neither; the headline; the
  subtext; the CTAs (one primary, at most one secondary). **Banned inside the hero:** a
  tagline under the CTAs, a trust micro-strip, a pricing teaser, a feature bullet list, an
  avatar row, floating stamp or badge icons, pills under the copy, raw stats, a version
  label, a decoration text strip at the bottom, a scroll cue. Each has a row in 15.24.
- **The logo wall lives under the hero**, as its own section, never in the hero's flex row.
- **A hero has a real visual** (15.23). Text beside a gradient blob is a placeholder.
- **The architecture is chosen, not defaulted.** Pick from the hero paradigms in 15.27 against
  the read. The centered-hero-over-dark-mesh and the left-text-right-image split are the
  two reflexes; either may be right, and each needs the one-line reason the plan records.
  A centered hero is the honest choice for a manifesto or launch page where the message is
  the design; the split is the honest choice for one strong asset beside one strong message.
- **It reads on a small laptop.** One focal point, obvious hierarchy, nothing competing. Do
  not try to expose the whole product in the first screen.

---

### Navigation

- **One line at the `lg` breakpoint.** When items do not fit, condense labels, drop
  secondary items, or move to a menu. A two-line desktop nav is broken design.
- **Height at most 80px on desktop**, 64-72px by default. No agency bar eating a sixth of
  the viewport.
- The current page is indicated. No link points at `#`. A floating pill or island nav is a
  direction choice (15.27), not a default.
- The mobile menu is a real menu (slide-in or full-screen), with a labelled trigger.

### Sections and rhythm

- **Every section has a job**: hook, proof, educate, convert. The default packs, before a
  brief edits them: **4 sections** (hero, features, social proof, CTA); **8 sections** (hero,
  trust bar, features, product showcase, benefits or use cases, testimonials, pricing,
  CTA); **12 sections** (hero, trust bar, feature grid, product preview, problem and
  solution, benefits, workflow, metrics or proof, testimonials, pricing, FAQ, CTA with
  footer). The page closes with one strong CTA and one supporting trust cue.
- **Layout families are rationed.** A family (three-column image cards, full-width quote,
  split text and image, bento, marquee, stacked poster, gallery) appears at most twice on a
  page and never in two adjacent sections. Eight sections use at least four families. The
  section titled "Selected work" must not look like the section titled "What we do".
- **Zigzag cap.** Alternating left-image-right-text, then left-text-right-image, is the
  most generated rhythm there is. At most two consecutive image-and-text splits; the third
  is broken by a full-width section, a vertical stack, a bento, a single marquee or a
  different family.
- **The eyebrow budget.** An eyebrow is the small uppercase wide-tracked label above a
  section headline (`SELECTED WORK`, `THE HARDWARE`; in code, an uppercase-plus-tracking
  utility pair or `text-transform: uppercase` with positive `letter-spacing` on a small
  size). Generated pages put one above every headline and produce the same templated
  rhythm every time. The rule is mechanical: **at most one eyebrow per three sections,
  rounded up, the hero counting as one**, and never on two adjacent sections. A nine-section
  page has at most three. The design scout (15.29) counts them. When a section wants a
  label, drop it; the headline is enough, and the section's place on the page already
  categorises it. **Scope:** this budget is a marketing-surface rule. In product UI an
  overline that labels a metric, a panel or a form group is a label, governed by the spec's
  `overline` role, and a direction whose signature is the overline (`industrial-precision`,
  `neo-luxury`) spends its marketing budget on the hero and the openers, never on every
  section.
- **No section numbering.** `00 / INDEX`, `001 · Capabilities`, `06 · how it works`,
  `Step 1 / Step 2`, `Phase 01`, `Pass One`. A label names the topic in plain words or does
  not exist. If progression must be shown, the verb is the label: Install, Configure, Ship.
- **Split-header ban.** The "big headline left, small explainer paragraph floating right"
  section header is banned as a default. A section carries one focused message; when a
  headline genuinely needs an explainer, stack them, body under headline, measure capped
  near 65 characters. The split earns its place only when the right column carries a
  visual or an interactive element, not filler prose. The related tell is the floating
  top-right sub-text with no alignment to anything (`tell.layout.floating-corner-text`).
- **Rhythm varies, spacing does not wander.** Alternate density, image-to-text ratio,
  alignment, scale and background intensity across the page (lighter, then richer, then
  calmer). Keep section-to-section spacing even and controlled; a cramped section beside an
  empty one is the tell.
- **One framing move per section.** No cards inside cards inside cards, no giant rounded
  wrapper around every section, no dashboard compartments on a marketing page. Fewer,
  stronger containers; open layouts; alignment and space instead of enclosure.
- **No decorative micro-UI.** Pseudo-system markers (`00 orchestration layer`), decorative
  status pills, fake control tags, filler chips, tiny badges on everything. Cleaner
  headings, fewer labels, real hierarchy.
- **One copy register per page** (15.23).

### Bento and feature grids

- **Rhythm, not repetition.** Six stacked left-image-right-text rows are not a bento. Vary
  the composition: a full-width feature row, asymmetric tile sizes, a vertical break.
- **Exactly as many cells as there is content.** Three items are three cells (1+2, 2+1, an
  asymmetric trio); five items are five cells (2+3, 3+2, hero plus four). An empty cell in
  the middle or at the end means the grid was planned wrong: reshape it, never paste a
  blank tile. Use `grid-auto-flow: dense` so spans interlock, and verify the spans add up.
- **Background diversity.** A multi-cell grid cannot be six white-on-white cards with text
  inside. At least two or three cells carry a real image, a brand-appropriate gradient
  (15.23, never the AI purple), a pattern or a tinted surface. Cream-on-cream typography
  tiles read as generated even when the rest of the page is good.
- **Restraint.** Three to five intentional cells beat eight messy ones.
- **Three equal feature cards are banned** (`tell.layout.three-equal-cards`). A two-column
  zigzag (within the cap above), an asymmetric grid, a scroll-pinned sequence or a
  horizontal scroll replaces them.

### Cards, shape and depth (every surface)

- **Cards only when elevation communicates real hierarchy.** Otherwise group with a top
  border, a divider list or negative space. Above density 7, generic card containers are
  banned outright and data breathes in plain layout with hairlines.
- **Tinted shadows, one light source.** A shadow carries the hue of the surface under it;
  pure black on a light background is banned. Every shadow on a page agrees on where the
  light comes from.
- **Shape lock.** One corner-radius system per page: all sharp, all soft (12-16px), or
  pills on interactive elements and one container radius. A mixed system is allowed only
  when the spec's Shapes section states the rule ("buttons full pill, cards 16px, inputs
  8px") and it is followed everywhere. Round buttons on a square layout, or square cards on
  a pill-button page, is broken design.
- **Concentric radii.** When a container nests inside a padded shell, the inner radius is
  the outer radius minus the padding; a nested pair with the same radius reads as a mistake
  (15.27, the double bezel).

---

### Lists, tables and spec sheets

- **More than five items is a different component, not a longer list.** A default bullet
  list or a divider-separated stack is the lazy choice past five. Reach for: a two-column
  split with grouped items; a card grid with an image and label per item; tabs or an
  accordion when the items categorise; horizontal scroll-snap pills; a carousel for
  breadth (testimonials, capabilities); a marquee for things that need no individual
  attention, at most one per page (15.25).
- **Spec sheets.** A long product specification table with a hairline under every row is the
  generated default for cookware, hardware, apparel and craft briefs, and it is banned.
  Instead: a two-column card grid (name, large value, one-line "why it matters"); scroll-snap
  pills; three logical clusters with one soft divider each; or three or four hero specs as
  large tiles with the rest under a "full specifications" disclosure.
- **No `border-top` plus `border-bottom` on every row.** Pick one edge and use it sparsely.
- **No data dumps on a marketing page.** A 20-row publication table, a 30-row award list,
  a full pricing matrix: show the top three to five and link to the full list, use a
  carousel for breadth, or give the data its own page when the data is the product.
- **No filled-track progress bars as comparison visuals.** A number with a small icon, or a
  thin inline bar with no background track. Filled tracks are dashboard chrome.
- **Product data tables** follow the direction's table treatment (15.1, reusable moments):
  tabular numerics right-aligned, sticky header, zebra striping off, hover as a surface
  shift.

### CTAs and forms (every surface)

- **Every button passes the contrast check.** Text readable against the button fill at
  WCAG AA (4.5:1 body, 3:1 for large text). White button with white text, a `bg-white` CTA
  carrying `text-white`, a transparent button on the page background with no border: all
  banned. A ghost button over a photograph gets a scrim, a backdrop or a stroke.
- **No CTA wraps at desktop.** A label that wraps to two or three lines is a broken button.
  Shorten the label (three words at most for a primary, ideally one or two) or widen the
  button; never constrain a CTA's width artificially.
- **One label per intent.** "Get in touch", "Contact us", "Let's talk", "Start a project"
  and "Reach out" are one intent, and one label serves it everywhere on the page (nav, hero,
  footer). The same holds for "Try free" / "Get started" / "Sign up", and for "View work" /
  "See selected work" / "Browse projects". Two labels for one intent is a pre-flight fail.
- **One primary per viewport tier**, at most one secondary, and the secondary looks
  secondary (scale, outline, ghost), never a clone of the primary. Vary the CTA style across
  the page (pill, outline, underlined inline link with an arrow, a banner CTA, a caption
  CTA under a strong visual) while keeping one identity.
- **Every interactive element has hover, press, focus and disabled states** (15.3). Press is
  a physical push: a one-pixel translate or a 0.98 scale.
- **Forms.** Label above the input, helper text present in the markup even when empty, error
  text below the input, one consistent gap. Never a placeholder standing in for a label.
  Inputs, placeholders, focus rings, labels and errors all pass AA against the section
  background; light placeholders on a near-white form, or a white form on a white section,
  are banned. Validate on the client for emails, required fields and formats.

### Theme lock (every surface)

- **The page has one theme.** If the page is dark, every section is dark. A light warm-paper
  section sandwiched between dark ones, or a sudden `#111` block in the middle of a cream
  page, reads as a copy-paste accident; the reader feels they walked into another site.
  Tints within the theme family are fine (a slightly darker or lighter surface); a flip is
  not.
- **The one exception** is a deliberate colour-block device: one full theme switch, with a
  strong transition, once per page, named in the composition plan.
- **Set the theme once, at the root.** With a system that ships theming (Radix Themes,
  shadcn/ui), the theme is set in the layout or page root; no section overrides it.
- Dual-mode support and the token strategy behind it are in 15.26.

---

### Mobile collapse (every surface)

- **Explicit per section.** Every multi-column layout declares its under-768px fallback in
  the same component. "Tailwind handles it" is not a declaration.
- **Single column, full width, standard inline padding** below the `md` breakpoint for every
  layout above variance 3 (15.20). Overlaps, rotations and negative margins are removed on
  touch; they cause tap-target conflicts.
- **Horizontal overflow on a phone is a critical failure.** Wrap the page in an
  overflow-x guard so an off-screen animation can never introduce a scrollbar (15.26).
- **Tap targets at least 44px**, buttons full width on mobile where the direction allows,
  body text never under 14px, headlines scaled with `clamp()` and still readable at 375px.
- **Inline-typography images** (a photo set between words of a headline, 15.27) stack under
  the headline on mobile.
- **Test at the viewports 15.26 names** before calling the page done.

### Native screen sets

The composition rules above are written for the web page. A mobile screen set follows the
spec's `platform.native` block (15.3) and these:

- **Platform mode first.** iOS-native, Android-native or cross-platform neutral, chosen
  before the first screen and never mixed. iOS biases toward calm top areas, tab-bar
  clarity and restrained chrome; Android toward firmer component rhythm, app-bar behaviour
  and bottom navigation; cross-platform toward universal patterns and less ornament.
- **Safe areas and system regions are respected**: status bar, title region, bottom
  navigation, home indicator, sheet docking zone, gesture space. A screen is an app screen,
  not a poster.
- **The first screen is the cleanest.** One focal point, a one-to-three-line statement, one
  clear next action, no stats, chips or pills, no wall of text, no website hero inside a
  phone frame. Imagery behind text keeps a scrim or a fade.
- **Navigation is believable.** Tab bar for major sections, stack feel for drill-down,
  sheets for secondary tasks, segmented controls for local switching, one primary path
  that is never hidden.
- **Onboarding is not three identical slides.** Distinct screens, varied balance of image,
  text and CTA, short copy, no early rating prompt, no motivational filler.
- **No box in box in box.** No five levels of framing, no widget wars on the home screen,
  no decorative pills or fake operating-system labels.
- **Not always simple; always clean.** A screen may be rich, layered and expressive when it
  stays readable; noisy complexity and clutter disguised as creativity are not allowed.
- **Text is never too small.** When the type feels small, the design is not finished:
  simplify, cut content, add space, enlarge, or split the content across two screens.
- **Category bias.** Fintech wants trust, calm spacing, clear numbers and no chart spam;
  health and fitness want a strong metric hierarchy in airy space; productivity wants list
  and card discipline; social wants feed and profile rhythm with real media moments;
  commerce wants browse, detail and cart clarity on stable product frames; wellness wants
  soft materials and breathing room.
- **A multi-screen set locks a design bible** (15.31) so screen four still belongs to the
  same product as screen one.
