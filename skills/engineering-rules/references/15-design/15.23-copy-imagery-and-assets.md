# 15.23 [law] Copy, imagery and assets

## The words and the pictures are part of the design

A page with perfect tokens and generated copy reads as generated. This file binds every
visible string, every image slot, every logo, icon and emoji on a UI surface. It is read at
Phase 3 before the first string is written, and the copy self-audit below runs before any
UI-bearing task ticks Phase 4. The tells it names carry ids in 15.24.

---

### Content density, the default shape of a section

A marketing page lives on the first impression, not the full read. Cut ruthlessly.

- **Default per section:** a headline of at most 8 words, a sub-paragraph of at most 25
  words, and one visual asset or one CTA. More than that must be justified by the section's
  job in the composition plan (15.22).
- **A page is not a document.** Lists past five items, tables past five rows and matrices
  belong in a different component or on a different page (15.22, lists).
- **Long paragraphs are for editorial surfaces only**, where the direction (`editorial-print`)
  sets the measure and the rhythm.

---

### The copy self-audit, mandatory before ship

Before declaring any UI-bearing task done, re-read **every visible string** on the touched
surface: headlines, subheads, eyebrows, button labels, body copy, captions, alt text, footer
text, empty-state text, error messages. Flag any string that is:

1. **Grammatically broken** ("free on its past", "two plans but one is honest", a phrase
   lifted out of the context that made it a sentence).
2. **Carrying an unclear referent** ("we plan to stay that way" with no prior "that").
3. **A hallucinated flourish**: cute-but-wrong wordplay, a metaphor that does not track, an
   "elegant nothing" phrase that sounds like meaning.
4. **A model trying to sound thoughtful**: passive-aggressive humility, fake-craftsman
   labels, mock-poetic micro-meta ("Each of these is a feature we ship today, not a roadmap
   promise. The list will stay short on purpose.").

Rewrite every flagged string. When unsure whether a string makes sense, replace it with a
plain functional sentence. **Generated cute copy is worse than boring copy.** The audit's
result is one line in the Evidence Ledger: `copy self-audit: <n> strings read, <n>
rewritten`.

---

### Numbers

- **A number is real, labelled mock, or absent.** `92%`, `4.1×`, `48k`, `5.8 mm`, `13.4 lb`
  come from the brief, the brand or public data; or they are marked as sample data in the
  markup and the update log; or they do not appear. Invented engineering precision the brand
  never claimed is banned (`tell.copy.fake-precise-spec`).
- **No fake-perfect numbers.** `99.99%`, `50%`, `1234567`, `$100.00`. Real data is messy:
  `47.2%`, `$99.00`, `+1 (312) 847-1928`.
- **Three identical stat columns** ("99% satisfaction, $10 saved, ∞ scale") appear only when
  the user asked for KPIs. Proof stays human otherwise: quotes, receipts, timelines,
  screenshots of real workflows.
- **Numbers in product UI** are set in the `numeric` role with tabular figures (15.3).

---

### Names, brands and avatars

- **No generic names.** "John Doe", "Jane Smith", "Sarah Chan", "Jack Su". Use realistic,
  locale-appropriate names, and a different one for every person.
- **No startup-slop brand names.** "Acme", "Nexus", "SmartFlow", "Cloudly", "Flowbit",
  "Quantumly", "NovaCore". Invent contextual names that sound like a company someone
  registered.
- **No generic avatars.** No egg silhouette, no icon-library user glyph, no one photo reused
  for three people. Believable placeholders, unique per person, or a consistent illustrated
  style.
- **No identical dates** across a list of posts, and no lorem ipsum anywhere: write real
  draft copy.

---

### Register and voice

- **One copy register per page.** Technical mono ("47 tasks · 0.6 ctx-switches/day"),
  editorial prose and marketing punch do not share a composition unless the brand voice
  explicitly asks for it.
- **Concrete verbs.** "Elevate", "Seamless", "Unleash", "Next-Gen", "Revolutionize",
  "Game-changer", "Delve", "Tapestry", "In the world of…" are banned
  (`tell.copy.filler-verb`). Say what the thing does.
- **Plain functional labels.** "Testimonials", "Latest writing", "Now working on". Never
  "From the field", "Field notes", "Currently on the bench", "On our desks", "Loose plates"
  (`tell.label.poetic-section`). Never "Quietly in use at" or "Quietly trusted by"; write
  "Trusted by", "Used at", "Customers include", or let the logos speak
  (`tell.copy.quietly-trusted`). Never mock-humble asides ("We respect the French ones").
- **Errors are direct, success is calm.** "Connection failed. Try again." Never "Oops!".
  No exclamation marks in success messages. Active voice: "We couldn't save your changes",
  never "Mistakes were made".
- **Sentence case on headers.** Title Case On Every Header is a tell.

---

### Quotes and testimonials

- **At most three lines** of quote body. A landing-page quote is a snippet; cut the original.
  Very small footer-style testimonials may stretch the cap slightly; the spirit is "fits in
  a glance".
- **Attribution is name, role and optionally company.** Never a name alone ("- Sarah").
- **Real typographic quote marks** or none. Not straight ASCII quotes.
- **No em-dashes inside the quote** as a flourish; see punctuation below.

---

### Punctuation

- **The em-dash is banned in UI copy.** No `—` anywhere a user can read it: headlines,
  eyebrows, pills, body, quotes, attribution, captions, button text, alt text. The en-dash
  `–` is banned as a separator too; ranges use a hyphen (`2018-2026`, `€40-80k`). Use a
  period, a comma, a colon, parentheses, or restructure the sentence. A quote attribution
  uses a spaced hyphen or a line break. This is binary: one dash on a page fails the
  pre-flight (15.30) and the design scout (15.29) greps for it. The only permitted dash
  characters are the hyphen and a minus sign in a number.
- **The middle dot is rationed.** At most one `·` per line in a metadata strip. It is never
  the default separator for everything; line breaks, hairlines and columns are.
- **Decorative status dots are zero by default.** A coloured dot before a nav item, a list
  row, a badge or a label is a tell unless it conveys real semantic state (a live server, an
  availability flag), and then at most one per section.
- **No `<br>`-broken, italicised headline splits** ("for thirty<br>*years.*") as a design
  move. Headlines read naturally first.
- **Emphasis stays in the family.** To emphasise a word in a headline, use the italic or bold
  of the same face. A serif word injected into a sans headline (or the reverse) is amateur.

### Complete pages, no skeleton sections

The plugin's law already refuses half-finished work (`SKILL.md` 1.1). On a UI surface it
takes these shapes, all banned:

- A section rendered as a heading and a placeholder comment (`// rest of sections`,
  `// similar to above`, `// add more as needed`, `{/* TODO content */}`).
- The first and last section built and the middle described in prose.
- One card built and "repeat for the others" written beside it.
- A component whose body is `...`.

Count the deliverables in the request (sections, screens, components), lock that number in
the composition plan, build every one in full, and cross-check the count before reporting.
When output must stop, it stops at a clean boundary (the end of a section) and says which
sections remain, never by compressing the rest. The design scout greps for the placeholder
tokens; Phase 6's cleanup sweep (13.2, class 7) catches what it missed.

### Imagery

Landing pages and portfolios are visual products. A text-only page with fake-screenshot
divs is slop, and so is a "minimalist" page that is merely incomplete.

**Priority order for every image slot**, applied slot by slot:

1. **An image-generation tool, when one is available in the environment.** Use it to create
   section-specific assets at the section's aspect ratio: hero photography, product shots,
   textures, mood images. Do not skip it because hand-rolled CSS feels faster. The
   image-first workflow (15.31) governs how many and how they are analysed.
2. **A real image otherwise.** Brand or stock URLs the brief provides; open-licence sources
   when explicitly allowed; a seeded placeholder service (a descriptive seed such as
   `marrow-cookware-kitchen`, sized to the slot) as a dev-time stand-in. A seeded
   placeholder is a stand-in, never a shipped asset: **every slot filled with one is listed
   in the update log (16.13) as "needs a real asset at <placement>, <size>"**, and Phase 6's
   cleanup sweep already greps for `placeholder`.
3. **Last resort: a labelled slot and a sentence to the user.** A clearly marked empty slot
   (`<!-- TODO(owner): hero product photo, 1600x1200 -->`) and, at the end of the response:
   "This page needs real images at: <list>." Never a hand-drawn SVG illustration or a
   div-built fake screenshot in its place.

**Rules that hold at every priority:**

- **Even a minimal page needs real images.** An editorial, Linear-style page still carries
  two or three (hero, one product or lifestyle shot, one supporting image), in restrained
  black-and-white if the dial is low. A pure-text page is not minimalism.
- **The hero has a real visual.** Text and a gradient blob is a placeholder.
- **Div-built fake product UI is banned** (`tell.hero.fake-product-ui`): fake task lists,
  fake dashboards, fake terminal windows assembled from rectangles, fake version footers
  inside them (`v0.6.2-rc.1 · last sync 4s ago`). Show a real screenshot, a generated one, a
  real mini-component rendered live, or editorial photography instead.
- **Images sit in a system of frames.** Fixed aspect ratios per module, repeatable media
  blocks, one radius rule, stable proportions across similar sections. Random image sizes
  with no system are a tell.
- **Treat stock so it does not look stock.** A restrained filter (`grayscale`,
  `mix-blend-mode: luminosity`, a slight contrast lift, a tonal overlay) matched to the
  palette. Two distinct crops on a multi-section page (a macro product shot beside a
  contextual environment), never one silhouette repeated.
- **Images behind text keep the text readable**: a bottom-to-top fade, a side mask, a soft
  scrim under the headline and CTA. Raw image under text with no support is banned.
- **Backgrounds with confidence, when the brief allows atmosphere.** A full-bleed image with
  a tonal overlay, a duotone or graded photo, a palette-matched tonal gradient, a tactile
  material, a flat colour field: chosen deliberately, once or twice per page. Do not retreat
  to plain white by reflex, and do not force atmosphere onto a brief that asked for
  restraint.
- **Gradient discipline.** Allowed and encouraged when professional: low-chroma tonal
  gradients (ink to graphite, cream to sand), a single-hue grade behind a photograph, a soft
  vignette, a noise-textured gradient for depth, an editorial colour wash matched to the
  brand. Banned as slop: rainbow or mesh-blob gradients, the purple-to-blue default, the
  pink-to-orange "creator" default, neon edges and halos, gradient text as a shortcut to
  "premium", any gradient that competes with the imagery.
- **Charts appear only when the product logically needs them** (analytics, pricing, infra,
  observability). Everywhere else proof is human.

### Logos and social proof

- **Real marks, never text wordmarks.** A "Trusted by" wall uses real SVG logos. Source them
  from an icon set that carries brand marks (Simple Icons, or a devicon set for a tech
  stack), or from the brief. A row of `<span>Acme Co</span>` styled as logos is banned
  (`tell.asset.text-wordmark-wall`).
- **An invented brand gets an invented mark.** A monogram in a circle, a two-letter
  ligature, an abstract glyph, rendered inline and styled to the page. Plain text for an
  invented name looks generic.
- **Logo only.** No industry or category caption under each logo (no `Vercel` with
  `hosting` beneath it). The logo is the credibility; the label adds nothing. The brand
  name lives in the alt text.
- **Both modes.** Every mark renders in light and dark (single-colour through a theme
  variable, or a light and a dark file).
- **The wall lives under the hero** (15.22), as a section of its own.

---

### Illustrations and decorative SVG

- **Hand-rolled decorative SVG is strongly discouraged** as a default: custom illustrations,
  logos, marks drawn path by path. Acceptable only when the brief asks for it, when the mark
  is a single simple geometric shape or a wordmark in display type, or when the quality is
  certain. Otherwise generate an image or leave a labelled slot.
- **A consistent illustration style**, when illustration is the brief, is documented in the
  spec's Shapes section; illustration that fights the palette is an anti-tell in several
  directions (15.4).
- **Crosshairs, hairline grids and rotated vertical text as decoration** are banned unless
  they organise real content or the brief is explicitly agency-experimental.

---

### Icons

- **From a library, never hand-drawn.** Icon paths drawn from scratch are banned
  (`tell.asset.hand-rolled-icon`). When a glyph is missing, install a second family or
  compose from primitives.
- **One family per project**, a standardised stroke width (1.5 or 2), a consistent grid
  size, monochrome, inheriting the current text colour unless the spec says otherwise.
  Phosphor, HugeIcons, Radix Icons, Tabler and Heroicons are all fine first choices; a
  direction may name its own (light strokes for `neo-luxury`, filled weights for
  `minimalist` product UI). Lucide and Feather are the unconsidered default of generated
  pages and are acceptable only when the user asks for them or the project already depends
  on them.
- **No cliché metaphors.** A rocket for "launch", a shield for "security", a lightbulb for
  "idea". Reach for the less obvious glyph (a bolt, a fingerprint, a spark, a vault).
- **Emoji are discouraged everywhere in UI**: markup, visible text, alt text. Replace with a
  library glyph. Allowed only when the user explicitly asks for a playful, chat-style or
  social-native voice, and then sparingly and with intent.

---

### Page chrome that generated pages forget

Each is a tell in 15.24 and a row in the pre-flight (15.30):

- A branded favicon.
- `<title>`, a description and social sharing meta tags on every page.
- A skip-to-content link for keyboard users.
- A custom, branded 404 page.
- Privacy and terms links in the footer; a consent banner where the jurisdiction requires it.
- A way back from every page; no dead ends.
- A footer that serves the main paths and the legal links, not a four-column link farm.
- Semantic landmarks (`nav`, `main`, `article`, `aside`, `section`) rather than div soup;
  descriptive alt text on every meaningful image, empty alt only on decoration.
