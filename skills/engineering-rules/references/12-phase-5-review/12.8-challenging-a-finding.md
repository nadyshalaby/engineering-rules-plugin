# 12.8 [check] Challenging a finding

**Judge every finding before spending an edit on it. The default is to keep it.**

A finding is a claim, not a fact. But dropping a real defect costs far more than fixing a
phantom one, so the burden of proof sits on the refutation, and it sits there heavily.

## The asymmetry, stated as a rule

- **Uncertainty is never a refutation.** "I do not think that is reachable" keeps the
  finding. Only a counter-citation removes it.
- **A refutation needs a `file:line`.** The guard that makes it safe, the carve-out that
  exempts it, or the catalog rule it cites that does not say what it claims.
- **An Important or Minor finding dies on one refutation.**
- **A Critical finding dies only when BOTH lenses fail to sustain it**, each with its own
  counter-citation, and the two verdicts are reported separately so a reader can see which
  one held.

## The two lenses, in this order

1. **Reproduction.** Can you reach the described state? Find the entry point, the input, and
   the path. If you can reproduce it, the finding is upheld regardless of what the second
   lens says.
2. **Authority.** Does the rule the finding cites actually say what the finding claims? Open
   the rule. A misquoted or over-extended rule is a refutation on this lens.

## The four verdicts

| Verdict | Means |
|---|---|
| **UPHELD** | The claim survives. Cite the reachable entry point, or the real rule. |
| **REFUTED** | Cite the `file:line` guard, carve-out, or missing rule that makes it wrong — plus what would change your mind. |
| **ESCALATED** | Real, and worse than stated. Say why. |
| **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The finding points at something real but describes it wrongly. **This is not a verdict that closes the row** — it holds the row open until the finding is restated. |

## What a verdict does and does not decide

A refutation on a Critical finding is **evidence**, never the decision. Both lenses refuting
a Critical earns an adjudication; the row stays `accept`, and out of the fix pass, until
that adjudication rules and the user signs off.

## Adjudicating a doubly-refuted Critical

1. Take that one finding alone, with both counter-citations in hand, and read the live diff
   again for it: open both cited lines, and re-derive whether the guard or carve-out holds
   for THIS case, not for the class (1.5: a safety claim is about this case).
2. Return one word per lens, **CONCUR** (the refutation stands) or **REBUT** (the finding
   stands), each with its own `file:line`. Bare agreement is forbidden.
3. Put it to the user through the wizard tool: the finding in one sentence, the two
   counter-citations, your verdict, and the recommendation. Options: fix it anyway
   (Recommended when either lens rebutted), accept the refutation and record it, or defer
   with a reason.
4. Only the user's answer flips the row to `push-back` or `defer`. Until then it is
   `accept`, and it is fixed in the fix pass like any other Critical.

## Verification

1. Did you judge every finding, at every severity, and not just the severe ones? (yes / no)
2. Does every REFUTED verdict carry a `file:line` counter-citation? (yes / no)
3. For every Critical, did you report both lens verdicts separately? (yes / no)
4. Did you keep every finding you were merely uncertain about? (yes / no)
5. Did you leave every `NEEDS-RESTATEMENT` row open rather than closing it? (yes / no)
6. For every doubly-refuted Critical, did the adjudication run and the user sign off before the row moved? (yes / no)
