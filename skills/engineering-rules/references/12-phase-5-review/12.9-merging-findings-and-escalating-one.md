# 12.9 [check] Merging findings, and escalating one

## Merging findings into one table

After the checks and the challenge, produce **one table** the fix pass works from.

| # | Severity | Finding | Location | Verdict | Decision | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **De-duplicate on file, line and claim.** Two checks finding the same defect is one row,
  noting both.
- **Never merge two findings that share a location but not a claim.** They are two fixes.
- **Sort by severity, then by file**, so the fix pass touches each file once.
- **Every row carries its verdict from the challenge**, so a decision can be read against
  the evidence rather than against the original assertion.
- **Every row carries Evidence**: for `accept`, the commit-shaped one-liner of the fix; for
  `push-back`, the counter-citation; for `defer`, the follow-up reference and the user's
  sign-off; for `needs-restatement`, the part nobody could pin down (16.2).

The four decisions:

| Decision | When |
|---|---|
| **accept** | Fix it in this pass. |
| **push-back** | The finding is wrong, and the challenge produced the counter-citation that says so. Record both. |
| **defer** | Real, not fixed now, with explicit user sign-off and a recorded reason. |
| **needs-restatement** | Real but misdescribed. Stays open. Not a way to close a row you did not act on. |

**A Critical finding is never `defer` and never `push-back` on your own judgment.** It is
fixed, or it goes through the adjudication in 12.8 and then to the user through the wizard
tool. **The table closes empty**: every row `accept` and fixed, `push-back` with its
counter-citation, or `defer` with its sign-off. A `needs-restatement` row still open when
the fixes are done is restated by you from the finding's own text and rejoins the table; if
it still cannot be stated, it goes to the user as a written line, never silently dropped.

## Escalating a finding

Escalate when the finding is real, and either bigger than the change under review or in
conflict with something the plan assumed.

1. **State what is actually broken**, in one sentence, with the `file:line`.
2. **State the blast radius**: what else in the codebase shares the defect. Search for it;
   do not estimate.
3. **State the options**, with cost and risk on each — including doing nothing.
4. **Recommend one**, and say why.
5. **Ask the user**, through the wizard tool. An escalation that resolves itself was not an
   escalation.

Verification for an escalation:

1. Is the defect cited with a `file:line` and a quoted snippet? (yes / no)
2. Did you search for other instances rather than estimating the blast radius? (yes / no)
3. Are there at least two genuine options, one of them recommended? (yes / no)
4. Did you say what happens if nothing is done? (yes / no)
