# 0.1 [orientation] How to read this file

These are the engineering rules and the working method. They are not advice. Everything in
`SKILL.md` binds from the first prompt of every session, with no invocation and no exception.
Everything here is read at the moment the phase you are in calls for it, from disk, by you
or by a fork that returns the lines that apply (5.5), and binds from that moment.

## How the sections are organised

Each section carries a type tag saying what kind of thing it is. The tag table is in
`SKILL.md` under "How to read this skill" and is not repeated here.

## The shape of the work

Eight phases, in order, none silently skipped:

**1 Clarify → 2 Plan and gate → 2.5 Spec review → 3 Implement → 3b Debug (only when stuck)
→ 4 Verify → 5 Review → 6 Finish.**

Full mode runs all eight with one user gate between Plan and Spec review. Quick mode, the
default, runs 1, 3, 3b, 4, 5, 6 with no gate: the approach is restated in chat at the end of
Phase 1 and work starts on it. In both modes a phase that does not apply is ticked
complete with a one-line reason, never dropped in silence.

**Where a file goes and what it is called is law, not taste.** Section 2.2 is the file and
folder contract: it is read before you create anything, and it binds every path and every
name in the change.

## How the work is carried out

**That is your call.** These sections say what has to be true: what gets checked, what
counts as evidence, what order things happen in, what the report must contain. They do not
say how you organise the doing of it. Split the work however serves the task, use whatever
tooling you have, and take whatever route to a correct result you judge best.

What is not your call is the outcome: every check named here runs, every gate named here
holds, and every claim carries the evidence these rules demand.

## The rules read like they were written for a team, because they were

Where a section says "hand this over", "the report", "the brief" or "state it out loud", it
means the artifact exists and is written down, in the work-doc or in the chat, not that some
particular arrangement of workers produced it. The artifacts are the point; they are what a
reader, a reviewer, or your own next session actually has. If you do use helpers, every rule
binds each of them and you own the ledger, the scouts, the evidence and every claim; 5.5 says
what goes out to a helper and what never does.

## Section 17 is a droppable appendix

The last group is twelve complete, ready-to-drop `DESIGN.md` specs, one per direction in the
design library. Nothing outside group 15 points into them and nothing in the working method
depends on them. Delete `references/17-ready-made-specs/` and everything else keeps working;
group 15 still names all twelve directions and still tells you how to author a spec from the
contract.
